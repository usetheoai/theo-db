## What shipped

The DataFusion vectorized CustomScan executor over the M99 theodb_columnar TAM (M100), phases A-D:
- A — the async-in-C block_on executor over real columnar Arrow batches, under HeldInterrupts (de-risked the pillar's #1 hazard).
- B — projection pushdown (decode only the aggregated columns).
- C — planner create_upper_paths_hook CustomScan integration: a simple count(*)/sum(float8) over a columnar table runs vectorized in ONE plan (EXPLAIN shows the node), result-identical to a row-store.
- D — safety hardening (work_mem MemoryPool errors-not-panics + target_partitions=1 Send-pinning) + the measured OLAP benchmark.

## Measured evidence

- 300 pg_tests GREEN, zero regression (droplet pg17 / pgrx 0.19).
- EXPLAIN shows the Custom Scan node; count(*)/sum(measure) result-identical to heap.
- Benchmark: the vectorized CustomScan (531 ms) is 9.89x faster than the M99 row-at-a-time seqscan (5251 ms) on the same columnar data (docs/benchmarks/m100-datafusion-executor.{md,json}, 2M rows, 5 runs).
- council-rust-pgrx + council-benchmark sign-off = READY_TO_MERGE, zero blockers.

## Honest boundary

Slice-1 scope: count(*)/sum(float8) without GROUP BY/WHERE (type-matching cases). GROUP BY, WHERE + min/max skip-pruning pushdown, avg, sum(int/numeric) are follow-up slices. Gated behind theodb.enable_columnar_agg (default OFF). The gain is vs the M99 seqscan on columnar-resident data; heap is faster for a single narrow aggregate; NO superiority claim vs heap or AlloyDB in-core (Rule 5 / M73/M97).
